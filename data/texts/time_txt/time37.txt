The cryptocurrency market experienced extreme volatility that tested the faith of investors, shook regulatory frameworks, and forced the entire industry to confront fundamental questions about its legitimacy and long-term viability as a financial system. Bitcoin crashed from its all-time high of nearly sixty-nine thousand dollars to below sixteen thousand dollars before beginning a dramatic recovery that eventually saw it surpass seventy thousand dollars, demonstrating both the terrifying risk and extraordinary potential returns that characterized this entire asset class. The collapse of the FTX exchange and the subsequent criminal conviction of its founder Sam Bankman-Fried exposed the dangers of centralized exchanges operating without adequate oversight, regulation, or transparency in an industry that had prided itself on decentralization. Ethereum's transition from proof of work to proof of stake, known as the Merge, was a landmark technical achievement that reduced the network's energy consumption by over ninety-nine percent. Institutional adoption continued as major financial firms launched Bitcoin exchange-traded funds that made cryptocurrency accessible to traditional investors through familiar brokerage accounts for the first time. The ongoing debate about whether digital currencies represent the future of finance or an elaborate speculative bubble remained unresolved, with passionate advocates and skeptical critics on both sides completely unable to convince each other.
