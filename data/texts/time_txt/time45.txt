The rise of streaming platforms fundamentally transformed the entertainment industry, reshaping how content is produced, distributed, and consumed by audiences around the world who increasingly abandoned traditional cable television in favor of on-demand viewing. Netflix, Disney Plus, Amazon Prime Video, HBO Max, Apple TV Plus, and numerous other platforms competed fiercely for subscribers, investing billions of dollars in original content that ranged from prestige dramas and big-budget science fiction to animated series and documentary programming that catered to every imaginable taste. The binge-release model pioneered by Netflix changed viewer habits permanently, with entire seasons dropped at once encouraging marathon viewing sessions that could turn an unknown show into a cultural phenomenon overnight. However, the economics of streaming proved challenging as the initial growth phase slowed and platforms were forced to raise prices, introduce advertising tiers, and crack down on password sharing to achieve profitability rather than simply pursuing subscriber growth at any cost. The impact on traditional Hollywood was profound, with theatrical window for films shrinking dramatically and many movies that would previously have received wide theatrical releases going directly to streaming platforms instead. The writer and actor strikes highlighted the ongoing tension between creative workers and the corporations that profited from their labor.
