Squid Game became Netflix's most-watched series in the platform's entire history, exposing the brutal consequences of economic despair through deadly reimaginings of childhood games that served as both visceral entertainment and sharp social commentary on modern inequality. Created by Hwang Dong-hyuk, who had struggled for over a decade to get the project funded because studios considered the premise too dark for mainstream audiences, the show's massive global success vindicated his artistic vision completely. The four hundred and fifty-six desperate players who entered the game, each carrying debts and failures that made the promise of winning the enormous cash prize worth risking their very lives for, represented a cross-section of society crushed by circumstance. The visual design of the show, with its childlike colors and imagery juxtaposed against extreme violence, created a dissonance that reinforced the central metaphor of innocence corrupted by greed and desperation in modern capitalist society. Player sixty-seven's death was one of the most emotionally devastating moments in the series, a reminder that behind every game piece was a human being with hopes and dreams and relationships that deserved better. The VIPs who watched the games for entertainment added another layer of critique about the exploitation of the desperate for the amusement of the wealthy and powerful.
