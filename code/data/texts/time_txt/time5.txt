The conflict between Russia and Ukraine that escalated dramatically in February 2022 reshaped the entire landscape of global politics, triggering energy crises across Europe and shifting military alliances that had remained stable for decades. NATO expanded significantly as Finland and Sweden applied for membership, ending decades of careful neutrality in response to the growing threat of Russian aggression on the European continent. The economic sanctions imposed on Russia were among the most severe in modern history, affecting everything from banking and finance to aviation and international trade. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky emerged as a powerful symbol of resistance, his nightly video addresses rallying both his own people and the international community to support the defense of Ukrainian sovereignty and independence. The humanitarian toll was staggering, with millions of refugees fleeing their homes and entire cities reduced to rubble by relentless bombardment and missile strikes. The war also exposed the world's dangerous dependence on fossil fuels, accelerating conversations about renewable energy and achieving true energy independence across the continent. Grain exports from both nations were disrupted, contributing to food insecurity in developing nations across Africa and the Middle East that depended heavily on their agricultural products.
