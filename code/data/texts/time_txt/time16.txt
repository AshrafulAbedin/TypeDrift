Succession's final season was a masterclass in television storytelling that will be studied and discussed for years to come by anyone who cares about the craft of writing compelling, character-driven drama. The Roy siblings, Kendall, Shiv, and Roman, tore each other apart in their desperate quest for control of Waystar Royco, their deceased father's media empire, revealing in the process how deeply Logan Roy's toxic parenting had damaged each of them in different but equally devastating and irreparable ways. Jesse Armstrong's writing was razor-sharp throughout the entire season, balancing dark humor with genuine emotional devastation in a way that made viewers laugh and wince in the very same breath without missing a beat. The series finale, in which Kendall was ultimately denied the position he had spent four entire seasons fighting desperately for, was a devastating conclusion that felt both surprising and inevitable simultaneously, the hallmark of truly great storytelling. Tom Wambsgans ascending to the throne through Shiv's betrayal of her own brothers was a bitter twist that perfectly encapsulated the show's central thesis about how power corrupts everyone who comes near it. Jeremy Strong's performance as Kendall Roy was a tour de force of restrained anguish. The show served as sharp commentary on media monopolies, billionaire culture, and generational dysfunction.
