Formula One's rivalry between Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton captivated the motorsport world and extended far beyond traditional racing fans to become one of the biggest sporting narratives of recent years across all sports globally. Their championship battle in two thousand and twenty-one, which culminated in the controversial final lap at the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix where a safety car restart gave Verstappen the opportunity to overtake Hamilton and claim his first title, produced one of the most contentious conclusions in the sport's seven-decade history. Hamilton had dominated the race and appeared certain to claim a record eighth championship when a late crash brought out the safety car and changed everything in an instant. Race Director Michael Masi's decision to allow only the lapped cars between Verstappen and Hamilton to unlap themselves was widely criticized as a departure from established regulations that fundamentally altered the championship outcome. The subsequent seasons saw Verstappen and Red Bull achieve dominance rarely witnessed in Formula One, with the Dutchman winning nineteen of twenty-two races in one season. The Red Bull car was an engineering masterpiece approximately half a second per lap faster than anything else on the grid. The cost cap regulations had not yet achieved their intended effect of promoting closer competition between the rival teams.
