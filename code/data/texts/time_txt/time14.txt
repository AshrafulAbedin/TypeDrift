Climate change continued to accelerate throughout the early and mid twenty-twenties, with record-breaking heatwaves, devastating wildfires, catastrophic floods, and unprecedented storms affecting communities on every inhabited continent across the globe with increasing frequency and severity. The scientific consensus grew even stronger that human activity, primarily the burning of fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial agriculture, was driving global temperatures to levels not seen in millions of years of Earth's geological history. Ocean temperatures reached alarming new highs, contributing to the bleaching and death of coral reefs that support marine ecosystems responsible for the livelihoods of hundreds of millions of people in coastal communities worldwide. Arctic sea ice continued its long-term decline, with summer extents reaching near-record lows that accelerated the warming feedback loop as dark ocean water absorbed more solar radiation than reflective ice surfaces had. Major cities from Phoenix to Delhi experienced heatwaves that pushed temperatures beyond what the human body can safely endure, leading to thousands of heat-related deaths and overwhelming healthcare systems. The insurance industry struggled to cope with escalating costs of climate-related disasters, with some companies withdrawing from high-risk markets entirely. International negotiations continued producing ambitious targets, but the gap between promises and actual emissions reductions remained dangerously wide.
