Attack on Titan concluded with one of the most polarizing endings in anime history, sparking debates that will likely continue for years to come among fans and critics alike. Eren Yeager's transformation from a freedom-seeking boy into one of fiction's most complex antagonists was a masterclass in character development that Hajime Isayama crafted over more than a decade of careful serialization. The Rumbling, in which Eren unleashed millions of colossal titans to flatten the world beyond the walls, forced every character and every viewer to confront uncomfortable questions about morality, survival, and the cyclical nature of hatred that defines human civilization. Mikasa's final choice to kill the person she loved most in order to save humanity was devastating and beautiful in equal measure. The Survey Corps, once heroes fighting for freedom against mindless giants, found themselves battling their own former comrade in a war that blurred every line between right and wrong. Levi Ackerman, despite his severe injuries, fought on with the same quiet determination that made him one of the most beloved characters in anime. The themes of freedom versus security, individual choice versus collective responsibility, and the endless cycle of violence resonated deeply with audiences across every culture and language around the world.
